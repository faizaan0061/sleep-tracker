Resting Heart Rate

Resting heart rate (RHR) is one of the clearest signals of overnight recovery. A lower RHR generally reflects better cardiovascular fitness, and regular cardio lowers it over time by helping the heart pump more blood with each beat. A rise of 5 or more bpm above your personal baseline is an early warning sign of stress, illness, or incomplete recovery.

Sleep Target

Sleep is your most powerful recovery tool. During deep sleep, your body releases growth hormone, repairs muscle, and clears metabolic waste from the brain. Most adults need 7–9 hours to move through all stages of sleep and wake up fully restored.

Sleep and Wake Consistency

Your circadian rhythm, the body's internal 24-hour clock, controls when you feel sleepy and when key hormones are released. Going to bed and waking at the same times every day, including weekends, keeps that clock in sync. Irregular schedules fragment sleep, leaving you less rested even after the same number of hours.
