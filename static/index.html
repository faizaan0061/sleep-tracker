Why it matters

Three signals. One score.

Your body tells you how recovered it is every morning. You just need to know what to listen for.

Resting Heart Rate

Your resting heart rate (RHR) is one of the clearest signals of overnight recovery. A lower RHR reflects better cardiovascular fitness, and regular cardio lowers it over time. A rise of 5+ bpm above your baseline is an early warning of stress, illness, or incomplete recovery.

Sleep Duration

Sleep is your most powerful recovery tool. During deep sleep your body releases growth hormone, repairs muscle, and clears metabolic waste from the brain. Most adults need 7–9 hours — but consistency matters just as much as duration. Going to bed at the same time every night keeps your circadian rhythm calibrated and makes those hours count.

Cardio Duration

30–90 minutes of cardio the previous day is the sweet spot for lowering your sleeping heart rate and improving sleep depth. Beyond 90 minutes, excess cortisol from overtraining can actually disrupt sleep and raise your resting heart rate the next morning — working against everything you're trying to build.
